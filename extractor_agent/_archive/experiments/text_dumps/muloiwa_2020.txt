Unstructured models
Kinetic growth models are useful because they assist engineers to
design and control biological processes (Dong et al., 2015). In the literature reviewed, there are 2 types of unstructured kinetic growth
models, those that account for substrate inhibition and non-substrate
inhibition (Annuar et al., 2008). Table 1 shows a list of the kinetic
growth models and description. The parameter constants of all the kinetic growth models are estimated using nonlinear least of squares.

The Monod model was ﬁrst introduced by Jacques Monod in 1942
to describe the relationship between speciﬁc growth rate and substrate
utilization rate in a bioreactor (Monod, 1949). The model can be deﬁned in two forms: based on substrate concentration only, and based on
both substrate and biomass concentration. The two forms of the model

limitation is observed at low substrate concentration. At low substrate
concentration, growth is dependant on substrate concentration. 3rd
limitation is that the Monod model cannot be applied when a substrate
exhibits inhibition (Kim et al., (2005); Shukor (2014); Han and
Levenspiel (1988)). Toxic substrate that can be found in industrial
wastewater can hinder bacteria growth and substrate consumption
(Shukor, 2014), and (Mahanta et al., 2014). The 4th limitation is that
the Monod model does not account for the fact that cells may require
substrate for maintenance during the death phase. The 5th limitation is
that the model does not account for the lag and death phase during the
growth phase (Kong (2017), and Mrwebi (2004)).

Haldane model
The Haldane model was introduced by Haldane in 1930. The model
is viewed as an extension of the Monod model. The model introduces a
3rd constant Ki that deals with speciﬁc growth rate inhibition at low
and high substrate concentration. Ki is the inhibition constant which is
equal to the highest substrate concentration at which the speciﬁc
growth rate is equal to one half maximum growth rate in the absence of
an inhibition. Kong (2017) reported that at high substrate concentration, speciﬁc growth rate of an organism may be hindered by the presence of toxic substrate. With the inclusion of the inhibition constant,
the model is capable of handling both toxic and non-toxic substrate. The
