Monod model
The Monod model was ﬁrst introduced by Jacques Monod in 1942
to describe the relationship between speciﬁc growth rate and substrate
utilization rate in a bioreactor (Monod, 1949). The model can be deﬁned in two forms: based on substrate concentration only, and based on
both substrate and biomass concentration. The two forms of the model
are deﬁned by Eqs. (3) and 4.

The
Monod
model
has
5
limitations
as
described
by
Kong (2017). The 1st limitation is observed at high substrate concentration. At high substrate concentration, the maximum speciﬁc
growth rate is independent of the substrate concentration. The 2nd
limitation is observed at low substrate concentration. At low substrate
concentration, growth is dependant on substrate concentration. 3rd
limitation is that the Monod model cannot be applied when a substrate
exhibits inhibition (Kim et al., (2005); Shukor (2014); Han and
Levenspiel (1988)). Toxic substrate that can be found in industrial
wastewater can hinder bacteria growth and substrate consumption
(Shukor, 2014), and (Mahanta et al., 2014). The 4th limitation is that
the Monod model does not account for the fact that cells may require
substrate for maintenance during the death phase. The 5th limitation is
that the model does not account for the lag and death phase during the
growth phase (Kong (2017), and Mrwebi (2004)).

to the Monod model, Blackman model, and the Moser model. In the
Contois model, the assumption is that biomass concentration is inversely proportional to speciﬁc growth rate. Just like the Monod model,
Blackman model, and Teissier model, the Contois model cannot describe the lag and death phase. Hence it does not capture substrate
inhibition. The Contois model has not been widely used to describe
speciﬁc growth rate, and when used, it produced satisfactory ﬁt on the
experimental data (Mrwebi, 2004). Abdullah et al., (2006) reported a
satisfactory ﬁt on Aspergillus oryzae in industrial natural rubber eﬄuent
serum. The Contois model produced R² of 80.2%. Ahmad et al., (2015)
reported a satisfactory ﬁt on ethanol production by Saccharomyces
cerevisiae. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria
growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 95.8%. Sun et al.,
(2009) reported satisfactory ﬁt on biodegradation of pharmaceutical
wastewater. The 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates.
The Contois model produced R² of 98.4%. On the other hand,
Ardestani (2011) reported a poor ﬁt on cell growth Pseudomonas putida
ATCC 16,024. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the
bacteria growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 75%.

growth. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria
growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 75.1%. The Contois
model performed well when it was ﬁtted on experimental data of different substrate concentration. Although the Contois model has been
described as not being capable of handling cell growth inhibition, literature reported above showed that it was capable. The Contois model
