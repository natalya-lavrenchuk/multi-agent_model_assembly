The Webb model was introduced by Edward in 1963 and is an unstructured model that describes kinetic growth based on substrate concentration only ( Webb, 1963 ). The Webb model describes specific growth rate as a function of substrate concentration only. The model is a modified version of the Haldane model. Although the aim of the Webb model was to improve to Haldane model, Dey and Mukherjee (2010) reported that it did not improve much. It was also evident in the literature that the improvement to the Haldane model did not achieve much. Halmi et al., (2014), reported poor fit of the model on experimental data of the Catechol-degrading Candida parapsilopsis. Dutta et al., (2015) reported the Webb model is similar to the Yano Koga model. The difference between the 2 models is the term (1 + ) S Ki . In the Webb model the term is in the numerator and in the Yano Koga model it is in the denominator. The same observation can be said about the Haldane and the Webb model. The Haldane model does not have the term (1 + ) S Ki . The Webb model is defined by Eq. (17) (Dey and Mukherjee, 2010) .

A number of authors have used to Webb model with success to describe microbial kinetic growth rate. Gharibzahedi et al., (2013) reported satisfactory fit on Canthaxanthin biosynthesis by Dietzia natronolimnaea HS-1 on waste molasses hydrolysate. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Webb model produced R² of 98.3%. Dutta et al., (2015) reported satisfactory fit on substrate inhibition growth kinetics for Cutinase producing Pseudomonas cepacia using Tomato peel extracted cutin. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Webb model produced R² of 93.3%. Choi and Lee (2018) reported a satisfactory fit on the bioenergy production with agricultural by products. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Webb model produced R² of 93.6%. Mohanty et al., (2018) reported a poor fit sulphate reduction by mixed microbial culture. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Webb model produced R² of 63%. Krishnan et al., (2017) reported satisfactory fit on the degradation of 3 Azo dyes RBRX-3B, DB-6 and DB-19. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Webb model produced R² of 99.9, 99.9, and 87.8% respectively. Abdussamad et al., (2017) reported satisfactory fit on lead concentration. The Webb model produced R² of 83%. The Webb model performed well when fitted to both toxic and non-toxic substrate. The Webb model struggled to fit experimental data on the effects of sulphate reduction reported by Mohanty et al., (2018) , while the Haldane model fitted the data with ease. Based on the

literature reported above, it could be agreed that the Webb model did

not improve much on the Haldane model Dey and Mukherjee (2010). Conclusion Literature shows that there are a number of unstructured models that can be used to describe kinetic growth rate. Based on the information from the literature, all the kinetic growth models were capable of describing growth rate. The only distinction is on the conducting of experiments. The collection of data, analysing of data, and environmental conditions will have an impact on the performance of each kinetic growth model. The most notable model the performed well was the Haldane model. The model was the most reported model in the literature with success on different types of substrate. The Monod, Moser, Teissier, and Contois model struggle to describe the lag and death phase during growth however they performed well as reported in the literature. The Logarithmic model was reported in the literature which concludes its incapacity to describe kinetic growth rate. The other important issue to take into consideration is the reliability of the results, sampling methods, analysing methods, and the degree of accuracy when conducting experiments. In experimental work, data is sometimes not accurate and this could be attributed primarily to the experimental cultivation systems used to collect the data, and poor selection and sensitivity of analytical methods for measuring biomass and substrate concentration.
