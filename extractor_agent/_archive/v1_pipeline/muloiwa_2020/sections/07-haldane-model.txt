The Haldane model was introduced by Haldane in 1930. The model is viewed as an extension of the Monod model. The model introduces a 3rd constant Ki that deals with specific growth rate inhibition at low and high substrate concentration. Ki is the inhibition constant which is equal to the highest substrate concentration at which the specific growth rate is equal to one half maximum growth rate in the absence of an inhibition. Kong (2017) reported that at high substrate concentration, specific growth rate of an organism may be hindered by the presence of toxic substrate. With the inclusion of the inhibition constant, the model is capable of handling both toxic and non-toxic substrate. The Haldane model is defined by equation 6.

The advantage of the Haldane model is that it is capable of describing all growth phases: lag, exponential, stationary, and death phase (Dutta et al., 2015 ). Due to its ability to describe growth rate at low and high substrate concentration, the Haldane model was the popular model used by many researchers ( Shukor (2014) ; Dutta et al., (2015) Dey and Mukherjee, 2010) .

The Haldane model has been hardly found waiting when being fitted to any experimental data. Wu et al., (2018) reported a satisfactory fit on kinetics and electro genesis change during cyanide degradation in a microbial fuel cell. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² of 98.6%. Saravanan et al., (2008) reported satisfactory fit on the growth kinetics of an indigenous mixed microbial consortium during phenol degradation. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced RMSE of 0.2020. Ibrahim et al., (2016)

reported a satisfactory fit on growth kinetic studies of caffeine-degrading bacterium. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 90%. The results reported by Ibrahim et al., (2016) differed from the one reported by Ahmad et al., (2015) . Ahmad et al., (2015) reported a poor fit on biodegradation of Caffeine. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 74.4%. Mohanty et al., (2018) reported satisfactory fit on effects of phenol on sulphate reduction by mixed microbial culture. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 81%. Halmi et al., (2014) reported a satisfactory fit on catechol-degrading candida parapsilosis. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 99.6%.

Hamitouche et al., (2012) reported satisfactory fit on the biodegradation of phenol by mixed culture. The growth rates were measured using Optical density (OD) at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 87%. Dey and Mukherjee (2010) reported a satisfactory fit on the performance and kinetic evaluation of phenol biodegradation by mixed microbial culture. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced RMSE of 0.0067. Debasmita and Rajasimman (2013) reported satisfactory fit on biodegradation of atrazine using mixed microorganisms. The growth rates were measured using OD at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 90%. The Haldane model produced R² 98%. Choi and Lee (2018) reported a satisfactory fit on micro-algal biomass/bioenergy production with agricultural by-products. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 89.9%. Carrera et al., (2004) reported satisfactory fit on kinetic models for nitrification inhibition by ammonium and nitrite in a suspended and an immobilised biomass systems. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 93.7%. Gharibzahedi et al., (2013) reported a satisfactory fit on cell growth and canthaxanthin biosynthesis by Dietzia natronolimnaea. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 93.1%. Abubakar et al., (2017) reported a satisfactory fit on growth kinetics of Tributytin-Resistant Klebsiella sp. FIRD 2 in cadmium Media. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 92.6%.

Abdussamad et al., (2017) reported a satisfactory fit on growth kinetics of Klebsiella sp. FIRD 2 on TBT-Resistant containing Lead. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Haldane model produced R² 96%. Shukor (2014) and Kong (2017) reported a satisfactory fit on Bacillus sp containing chromate. The Haldane model produced RMSE and R² of 0.0004 and 95.6% respectively. Raghuvanshi and Babu reported a satisfactory fit on the removal of methly ethly-ketone substrate. The Haldane model produced R² 87%.
