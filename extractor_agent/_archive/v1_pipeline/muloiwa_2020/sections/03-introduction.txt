In biological wastewater treatment process, bacteria are used to oxidise dissolved and particulate carbonaceous organic matter into simple end products. The organic matter present is composed of domestic and industrial waste and is usually referred to as substrate ( Choi et al. (2017) , and Metcalf and Eddy, (2003)). In addition to substrate, oxygen is required to complete biological wastewater treatment process. The biological wastewater treatment process can be expressed by reaction equation 1.

Bacterial growth can be described in 4 phases: Lag phase, Exponential phase, Stationary phase, and death phase. In the lag phase, bacteria take time to acclimatize to their new environment in the bioreactor. While the acclimatizing process takes place, there is no growth of bacteria in the bioreactor. The exponential growth phase is the most rapid growth possible. During this phase, the bacteria present in the bioreactor doubles at a high rate. The stationary phase defines

the no growth phase. Although there is no growth in the stationary phase, the bacteria continues to multiple while some continue to die. The stationary phase occurs due to the depletion of essential nutrients, and presence of toxic substrate. The last phase is the death phase. In the death phase, nutrients required for bacterial growth are completely used up. The 4 phases are described in Fig. 1. ((Metcalf and Eddy, (2003), Maier and Pepper (2015) ).

Bacteria growth is determined using Eq. (2) . Eq. (2) requires the measurement of biomass concentration at different time intervals. There are two common methods used to determine biomass concentration: Cell dry weight mass method and the Optical density (OD) method. The cell dry weight mass method has the advantage of providing direct unconditional estimates. The disadvantage of the cell dry weight mass method is interference from dead cells and non-cell solids. Further-more the method is time consuming and labour intense ( Panikov, 2009 ). The OD method has the advantage of its simplicity, quickness, and automation. The disadvantage of the OD method is the interference with non-cell solids. The OD method measures the absorbence of the sample using a spectrophotometer at a wavelength between 540 - 600 nm. A calibration curve of absorbence and cell dry weight mass is required to relate absorbence with cell dry weight mass. In other words cell dry weight mass method for each culture is required at least once when using the OD method. OD measurements are then converted to biomass concentration using the calibrated curve ( Panikov (2009), and Wang (2009)) .

concentration. There are different types of kinetic growth models used to describe specific bacteria growth rate in biological wastewater treatment process. These methods can be further divided into structured and unstructured kinetic growth models. Structured kinetic growth models incorporate genetic, morphological, or biochemical attributes that collectively determine the physiological state of the biomass. Although structured kinetic growth models are capable of describing specific growth rate of the bacteria, they are complicated and they require a number of equations to solve (Dong et al., 2015 ; Mrwebi (2004); Panikov (2009)) . Unstructured growth models view bacteria as independent organisms which interacts with the environment. Unstructured kinetic growth models describe bacteria growth rate based on biomass and substrate concentration ( Dong et al., (2015) ; Mrwebi (2004); Panikov (2009)) . Unstructured kinetic growth models are more suitable for steady state balance while structured models are more suitable for unsteady state balance ( Panikov, 2009) .

One of the earliest kinetic growth models used to describe specific growth rate is the Monod model (Monod, 1942). Later on, it was found that the Monod model was not capable of describing specific growth rate in the presence of toxic substrate concentration. (( Haldane, 1965 ), ( Luong, 1987) , and ( Aiba et al., 1968) ). At high substrate concentration, nutrients inhibit the growth of bacteria due to toxic substrate

concentration (Alqahtani, 2013 ). Examples of toxic substrate include: cadmium, phenols, ethanol, volatile acids, nitrates, ammonia, and thiocyanates ( Alqahtani, 2013 ). The kinetic growth models were evaluated using coefficient of determination (R²). R² is defined as the ratio of the explained variation to the total variation. Gharibzahedi et al., (2013) explained that R² is used to determine the efficiency of the model and its closeness to a value of 1 is an effective and practical measure of the validity of a model prediction. On the contrary, Ford, (2015) Statistical Research Consultant at the University of Virginia reported that R² cannot be used as a measure of goodness to fit, predictive error, and cannot be used to compare models. One of the error functions used to check the robustness of a model is by determining root mean square error (RMSE). RMSE is the standard deviation of the residual. Residuals are a measure of how far from the regression line data points are ( Sharifahmadian, (2015) Chai and Draxler (2014) ). This paper aims to review the performance of 14 unstructured kinetic growth models that are used to describe specific bacteria growth rate. The models reviewed are: Monod, Blackman, Haldane, Teissier, Moser, Contois, Logarithmic, Powell, Han and Levenspiel, Logistic, Luong, Webb, Yano and Koga, and Aiba-Edwards.
