The Blackman model was introduced by Blackman in 1905 ( Blackman, 1905 ). The model is similar to the Monod model in terms of its assumptions. At low substrate concentration, growth is dependant on substrate and at high substrate concentration, growth is independent of substrate concentration. At high substrate concentration, some nutrients are limiting ( Kong, 2017) . Just like the Monod model, the Blackman model is unable to capture the lag and death phase, and in turn it over estimates the specific growth rate ( Kong, 2017) . The Blackman model is not a popular method for modelling growth rate in microbial processes, and when utilized it performed poorly. Mahanta et al., (2014) reported results on the comparison between Blackman, Monod, Powell, and Moser models. In the study the Blackman model was the only model that did not fit the experimental data with a reported R² of 43.3%. The Blackman model has been shadowed by models such as the Monod model.
