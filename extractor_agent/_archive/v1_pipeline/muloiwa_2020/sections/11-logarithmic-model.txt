The logarithmic model was introduced by Westerhoff et al., (1982) . The model describes specific growth rate as a function of logarithm of substrate concentration. The Logarithmic model is the worse model when compared with models discussed above because it is not capable of describing the lag, stationary, and death phase. The model has a reputation of over estimating cell growth, and if the substrate concentration is low, it can produce negative growth rate ( Kong, 2017 ). This is a set back because growth cannot be classified as negative, and hence the Logarithmic model is not well represented in literature. The
