The Monod model was first introduced by Jacques Monod in 1942 to describe the relationship between specific growth rate and substrate utilization rate in a bioreactor (Monod, 1949 ). The model can be defined in two forms: based on substrate concentration only, and based on both substrate and biomass concentration. The two forms of the model are defined by Eqs. (3) and 4.

The maximum bacteria growth rate and half saturation constant are constants. The Monod model assumes that there is only one growth limiting substrate in the bioreactor (Monod, 1942). The Monod model has 5 limitations as described by Kong (2017). The 1st limitation is observed at high substrate concentration. At high substrate concentration, the maximum specific growth rate is independent of the substrate concentration. The 2nd limitation is observed at low substrate concentration. At low substrate concentration, growth is dependant on substrate concentration. 3rd limitation is that the Monod model cannot be applied when a substrate exhibits inhibition ( Kim et al., (2005); Shukor (2014); Han and Levenspiel (1988)) . Toxic substrate that can be found in industrial wastewater can hinder bacteria growth and substrate consumption ( Shukor, 2014) , and (Mahanta et al., 2014 ). The 4th limitation is that

the Monod model does not account for the fact that cells may require substrate for maintenance during the death phase. The 5th limitation is that the model does not account for the lag and death phase during the growth phase (Kong (2017), and Mrwebi (2004) ).

The limitations described above were evident on literature reported below. Hamitouche et al., (2012) reported a poor fit on biodegradation of phenol by mixed culture in a batch reactor. The growth rates were measured using Optical density (OD) at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The model produced R² 52%. Debasmita and Rajasimman (2013) reported a poor fit on biodegradation of atrazine using mixed microorganisms. The growth rates were measured using OD at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The model produced R² 30.4%. Ahmad et al., (2015) reported a poor fit on biodegradation kinetics of Caffeine by Leifsonia sp. strain SIU. The model produced R² 47.9%. Abubakar et al., (2017) reported a poor fit on growth kinetics of Tributytin-Resistant Klebsiella sp. FIRD 2 in Cadmium Media. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The model produced R² 53.4%. Abdullah et al., (2006) reported a poor fit on cell growth kinetics of Aspergillus oryzae in industrial natural rubber effluent serum. The model produced R² 65.3%. Abdussamad et al., (2017) reported a poor fit on growth kinetics of Klebsiella sp. FIRD 2 on TBT-Resistant containing Lead. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The model produced R² 75%. Ardestani and Kasebkar (2018) reported a poor fit on Aspergillus niger growth and substrate uptake in a batch submerged culture. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The model produced R² 77.7%. It was expected of the Monod model to struggle with describing kinetic growth rate on the literature above because the substrate utilised have been described to exhibit inhibition of cell growth (Ku et al., 2011 ; Hashimoto et al., 2004) .

Despite the poor fit of the Monod model on the literature mentioned above, the model still remains the most widely used to describe specific growth rate and substrate utilization rate ( Rajasekar et al., (2015) , Ravi et al., (2013); Dong et al., (2015); Rodríguez-Gómez et al., (2014)) .
