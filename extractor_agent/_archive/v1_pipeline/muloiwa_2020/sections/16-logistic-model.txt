The Logistic model was introduced by Pierre Verhulst in 1838 ( Verhulst, 1838 ). The Logistic model is also known as Verhulst model ( Omer, 2018) . The model is based on an assumption that the growth rate of an organism is proportional to the current population, and the unutilized resources in a closed habitat (Horowitz et al., 2010) . The model is a substrate independent model, it describes kinetic growth based on biomass concentration only. The model accounts for inhibition of substrate (Ali et al., 2017 ). A number of authors have used the Logistic model to predict specific growth rate. Ardestani and Kasebkar (2018) reported a poor fit in Aspergillus niger growth. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The model was produced R² of 67.7%. Abdullah et al., (2006) reported poor fit of Aspergillus oryzae in industrial natural rubber effluent serum. The Logistic model produced R² of 20.9%. Ardestani and Sha fiei (2014) reported a satisfactory fit on the cell growth of Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Logistic model produced R² of 97%. Ali et al., (2017) reported satisfactory fit of the Logistic model on ethanol production from inulin by Pichia caribbica. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The model produced R² of 91%. The performance of the Logistic model was inconsistent as reported in the literature above. Although the model showed its ability to handle toxic substrate, it was never convincing. The lack of literature on the performance of this model makes it difficult to draw a conclusion on its ability to describe kinetic growth.
