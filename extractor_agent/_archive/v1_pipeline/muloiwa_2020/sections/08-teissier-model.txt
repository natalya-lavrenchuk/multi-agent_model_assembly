The Teissier model was introduced in 1942 (Teissier, 1942) . The author is sometimes referred to as Tessier. The Teissier model is also known as the Exponential model that describes the specific growth rate as a function of continuous substrate concentration. The model is similar to the Monod model, and Blackman model. Experimental data fitted on the Teissier model was observed to lie in between the Monod model, and Blackman model ( Kong, 2017 ). Just like the Monod model, Blackman model, and Moser models, the Teissier model is not capable of describing cell growth inhibition. Hence it is not capable of describing the lag and death phase. The Teissier model differs with the Monod model, Blackman model, and Moser model when it transitions from first order kinetics to zero order kinetics ( Kong (2017); Dey and Mukherjee (2010) , and Dutta et al., (2015)) . Literature has shown that the Teissier model is capable of describing specific growth rate in different substrate concentrations.

Krishnan et al., (2017) reported a satisfactory fit on biodegradation kinetics of Azo dye Mixture. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 98.2%. Dey and Mukherjee (2010) reported a satisfactory fit on phenol biodegradation by mixed microbial culture in a batch reactor. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced RMSE of 0.0075. Sadhukhan et al., (2016) reported a satisfactory fit on microbial production of succinic acid using crude and purified glycerol. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced RMSE of 0.225. Choi and Lee (2018) reported a satisfactory fit on micro-algal biomass/bioenergy production with agricultural by-products. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 92.5%. Ahmad et al., (2011) reported a satisfactory fit on growth kinetic and modelling of ethanol production by Saccharomyces cerevisae. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 96.3%. Annuar et al., (2008) reported a satisfactory fit on the growth of Pseudomonas putida with ammonium as a growth limiting substrate. The 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Tesseir model produced R² of 95.6%. Sun et al., (2009) reported a satisfactory fit on the degradation of pharmaceuticals in wastewater treatment. The 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 99.5%. Abdullah et al., (2006) reported a satisfactory fit on Aspergillus oryzae in industrial natural rubber effluent serum. The Teissier model produced R² of 83.6%. The Teissier model performed well when subjected to substrate that exhibits cell growth such as Azo dye, phenol, ethanol, ammonium, and succinic acid.

On the other hand, Abdussamad et al., (2017) reported a poor fit on growth kinetics of Klebsiella sp. FIRD 2 on TBT-resistant containing Lead. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 42%. Abubakar et al., (2017) reported a poor fit on growth kinetics of Tributytin-Resistant Klebsiella sp. FIRD 2 in cadmium Media. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 59.5%. Ahmad et al., (2015) reported a poor fit on biodegradation kinetics of caffeine. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Teissier model produced R² of 68%. Debasmita and Rajasimman (2013) reported a poor fit on biodegradation of Atrazine using mixed microorganisms. The growth rates were measured using OD at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The Teissier model

produced R² of 31.5%. Although the Teissier model was able to fit experimental data on phenol, ethanol, and ammonium, it struggled with fitting data on substrate that exhibits cell inhibition such as lead, cadmium, caffeine, and atrazine. The performance of the model is unpredictable and it may rely on the sampling methods, and data analysing methods.
