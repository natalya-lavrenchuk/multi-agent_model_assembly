The Contois model takes a different approach when compared to the Monod model, Blackman model, and the Moser model. In the Contois model, the assumption is that biomass concentration is inversely proportional to specific growth rate. Just like the Monod model, Blackman model, and Teissier model, the Contois model cannot describe the lag and death phase. Hence it does not capture substrate inhibition. The Contois model has not been widely used to describe specific growth rate, and when used, it produced satisfactory fit on the experimental data ( Mrwebi, 2004 ). Abdullah et al., (2006) reported a satisfactory fit on Aspergillus oryzae in industrial natural rubber effluent serum. The Contois model produced R² of 80.2%. Ahmad et al., (2015) reported a satisfactory fit on ethanol production by Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 95.8%. Sun et al., (2009) reported satisfactory fit on biodegradation of pharmaceutical wastewater. The 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 98.4%. On the other hand, Ardestani (2011) reported a poor fit on cell growth Pseudomonas putida ATCC 16,024. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 75%. Ardestani and Kasebkar (2018) reported a poor fit on Aspergillus niger growth. The cell dry weight method was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Contois model produced R² of 75.1%. The Contois model performed well when it was fitted on experimental data of different substrate concentration. Although the Contois model has been described as not being capable of handling cell growth inhibition, literature reported above showed that it was capable. The Contois model handled ethanol, and industrial natural rubber as reported by Ahmad et al., (2015), and Abdullah et al., (2006). The two substrate types were reported to exhibit cell inhibition. The performance of the Contois model can be unpredicted as reported in the literature above.
