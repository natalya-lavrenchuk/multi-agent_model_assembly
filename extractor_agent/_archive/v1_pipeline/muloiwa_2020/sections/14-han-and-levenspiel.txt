The Han and Levenspiel model was introduced in 1988 as an extension of the Monod model. The extension includes inhibition effects that are present due to high concentration of substrate, cells, or other products (Han and Levenspiel, 1988) . The model is capable of explaining the types of inhibition as either competitive or non-competitive ( Dutta et al., 2015) . The inclusion of the parameter Sm increased the model's ability to predict cell growth in both toxic and non-toxic substrate concentration as reported in the literature below. Saravanan et al., (2008) reported satisfactory fit of the Han and Levenspiel model in phenol degradation. The growth rates were measured using OD at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The Han and Levenspiel model produced a RMSE of 0.0211. Dey and Mukherjee (2010) reported satisfactory fit on biodegradation of phenol by indigenous mixed microbial system. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Han and Levenspiel model produced a RMSE of 0.0218. Krishnan et al., (2017) reported satisfactory fit on biodegradation of Azo dye mixture. Only Halmi et al., (2014) reported a poor fit of the Han and Levenspiel model in Catechol degradation. The author did not even document the results (R² and RMSE) from the Han and Levenspiel model. The few literature reported above showed good performance of the Han and Levenspiel model to describe specific growth rate. The model was capable of handling both toxic and non-toxic substrate concentration.
