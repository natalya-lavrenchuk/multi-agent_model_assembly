The model has not been criticised of its inability to described kinetic growth rate. This in evident from the literature described below. Shukor et al. (2014) reported satisfactory fit on Tannery effluent containing chromate. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano and Koga model produced R² of 95.6%. Mohanty et al., (2018) reported a satisfactory fit on effects of phenol on sulphate reduction. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano and Koga model produced R² of 82%. Krishnan et al., (2017) reported a satisfactory fit on biodegradation of Azo dye. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano Koga model produced R² of 98.1%. Halmi et al., (2014) reported a satisfactory fit on biodegradation of catechol. The OD at 600 nm was used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano Koga model produced R² of 99.5%. Abubakar et al., (2017) reported satisfactory fit in cadmium media. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano and Koga model produced R² of 93.6%. Abdussamad et al., (2017) reported a satisfactory fit on the degradation of TBT-Resistant containing lead. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano and Koga model produced R² of 95%. Schröder et al., (1997) reported a satisfactory fit of the Yano and Koga model in the biodegradation of phenol. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano and Koga produced sum of square error of 0.000035. TAZDAIT et al. (2014) reported a study on the degradation of malathion. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. Yano and Kago model produced R² of 98%. Tazdaït et al., (2018) reported a satisfactory fit on the biodegradation of glyphosate with un-acclimated activated sludge. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The model produced R² of 99.9% ( Tazdaït et al., 2018) . Tazdaït et al., (2018) reported that the inhibition constants were higher than the usual reported values. Dey and Mukherjee (2010) reported satisfactory fit on the biodegradation of Phenol by indigenous mixed. The cell dry weight method and OD at 600 nm were used to measure the bacteria growth rates. The Yano and Koga model produced RMSE of 0.0079. The only author who reported a poor fit was Debasmita and Rajasimman (2013), who reported a poor fit on biodegradation of Atrazine using mixed microorganisms. The

growth rates were measured using OD at 600 nm to describe the specific growth rates. The Yano and Koga model produced R² of 38%. The literature above showed that the Yano and Koga model performed well and is a robust kinetic growth model that is capable of handling both toxic and non-toxic substrate with ease.
